Research programme

Exact algebraic and geometric structures in field theory, quantum geometry, and information geometry

Exact geometry as a generator of physics.

My research develops exact algebraic and geometric structures as tools for extracting concrete physics. Across Yang–Mills theory, electromagnetic knots, quantum geometry, finite spectral triples, black-hole phases, and quantum information geometry, the recurring mechanism is to begin with a rigid mathematical object and derive physically sharp outputs: exact fields, conserved charges, geometric phases, spectral distances, particle-sector structures, transport laws, and information-flow diagnostics.

Pillars Current focus Selected works Talks

Common mechanism

Rigid structure, exact computation, physical output.

The programme starts from conformal compactifications, homogeneous spaces, finite spectral triples, Clifford/octonionic/quaternionic algebra, quantum-Riemannian geometry, holonomy reductions, rational Maxwell bases, and Yang–Mills ansätze. The aim is not to force every problem into one formalism, but to hold each direction to the same standard: the algebraic input must be explicit, the geometry must be computable, and the physical interpretation must be earned by the calculation.

Programme pillars

Six ways exact geometry becomes physics

A

Exact Yang–Mills fields and geometric transport

Non-Abelian exact solutions, conformal transport, pulse structures, defect sources, AdS/lightcone geometries, and gauge-theoretic cosmological sectors.

B

Rational Maxwell fields and electromagnetic knots

Rational Maxwell bases, null and extremal sectors, Hopf/open-book structures, conformal charges, and probe trajectories in knotted electromagnetic backgrounds.

C

Quantum geometry of black holes and finite spaces

Quantum-Riemannian geometry, black-hole geometric phases, congruence flows, projective/UV quantization ideas, and finite quaternionic geometry.

D

Finite spectral triples and particle geometry

Finite noncommutative geometries, Clifford-algebraic structures, scalar sectors, link fields, and particle-sector model building.

E

Quantum information geometry

Spectral distances, Helstrom distinguishability, calibration geometry, information backflow diagnostics, anisotropic qubit metrics, and channel contractivity.

F

Holonomy and special geometric sectors

Special holonomy, characteristic connections, G2-type structures, instanton sectors, and regulated nonperturbative geometric quantities.
